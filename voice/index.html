    Talk to Amie¶

    Speak, and Amie does what you said or answers out loud. ./scripts/setup.sh puts everything in place once: about 4 GB of free ONNX models, the shell scripts and the template. Then Templates > Talk to Amie > Load, and Run. The script loads nothing until you type talk in the console.

    A companion on your Mac¶

    Amie has a face, greets you, remembers what you ask it to, and works the Mac through small jars with no keys. face.jar floats over your windows: it listens, thinks, talks and smiles along with the talk, and you can drag it anywhere. Close the app while it talks and it keeps going; the menu bar's face brings the window back or quits.

    Amie hears you out: microphone.jar records until you stop talking, up to 20 seconds. When the intent model is at least 98.5% sure it acts at once, which takes no time. Otherwise Llama reads your words and picks what to do, which takes a few seconds, and anything that makes no sense gets "Sorry, I didn't catch that" instead of an action. To teach it how you talk, add rows to voice-actions.csv, delete AMP/models/intents/amie-intents, and it trains again. To answer faster and hear a little less, set whisper=whisper-base.en in the Packages tab.

    The first use of each app makes macOS ask to let Amie control it, under System Settings > Privacy & Security > Automation. Mail and the calendar are read out as they are: only a chat or a catch-up goes through Llama, and text in an email can't trigger an action.

    What happens to what you say¶

    1. microphone.jar records five seconds, and Whisper turns them into text.
    2. The amie-intents BERT intent model picks an action. It trains itself from AMP/voice-actions.csv on the first Run. Under a score of 0.5 the words count as a question.
    3. The action runs: a script of AMP/pluginDir/voice, or Llama.
    4. The Supertonic voice speaks, and microphone.jar plays it.

    When the next turn starts, the console says where the last one's time went, from Whisper on, without the wait for you to speak: Took … ms: hearing … ms, intent … ms, Llama … ms, voice … ms, playing … ms. A part that didn't run that turn isn't listed, and Llama counts every question it was asked.

    Action For example What it does
    weather "What's the weather in London?" weather.sh, from wttr.in
    list_files "List the files in my downloads" list-files.sh

    To teach it another action, add rows to the CSV, delete AMP/models/intents/amie-intents, and add a branch to the when of the script.

    What Amie manages¶

    Llama, the intent model, Whisper, the voice and microphone.jar are sessions of the app: they are in Still open, under their own names, and Stop or Terminate ends them. initMod("whisper") and initMod("voice") open the ones speech.properties names, and a block such as ortClosable holds them like any model. Each loads when first used and stays until talking ends. Their memory is native, so the session shows what they weigh on disk.

    Which Whisper, which voice, how fast it talks and how many threads it uses are in speech.properties, and the microphone in microphone.properties. speaker picks who talks: 0 af, 1 af_bella, 2 af_nicole, 3 af_sarah, 4 af_sky, 5 am_adam, 6 am_michael, 7 bf_emma, 8 bf_isabella, 9 bm_george, 10 bm_lewis. Any Piper voice of sherpa-onnx also works if you put it beside them and name it in voice. Both open in the Packages tab. The models sit beside that file, in AMP/models/speech.

    When a command fails¶

    Each command goes through .patchBy(::explain). A command that fails hands what it said to explain, which asks Llama why and speaks the answer. patchBy takes any function, so the same error can go elsewhere:

    shellScript("voice/brew-install.sh", listOf("wget"))
    .patchBy(::log)                                  // the console
    .patchBy { error -> speak(error) }               // the voice, word for word
    .patchBy { error -> log(llama.answer(error)) }   // a model
    .patchBy(::handleBy)                             // the local text model, with main.kts and the env in view

    What a failed command printed is in the error terminal too, with why it failed: errorLog() pulls its last lines, and handleBy() hands them to the local text model, so a script can ask about the whole of what the console showed in red, not only the one command.

    textModel in the script picks the model: llama3.2-3b (about 3 GB of memory) or the faster qwen2.5-1.5b (about 1.3 GB). Other exports go in AMP/models/text. An export that needs a graphics card won't load here.

    In an exported bundle¶

    Export the template as any script. The intent model, the text model, the two packages, the speech models and the shell scripts all go along, and talk is read from standard input. speech.jar carries the native library of Apple silicon only, so the bundle runs on those Macs.
